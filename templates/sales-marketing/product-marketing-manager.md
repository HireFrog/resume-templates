# Product Marketing Manager Resume Template

*For marketers who own positioning, messaging, and go-to-market strategy for a product or product line, bridging Product, Sales, and Marketing.*

Product Marketing Manager (PMM) resumes are judged on strategic impact more than raw activity: can you position a product against the competition, run a launch that moves metrics, and arm Sales to win deals. This template is structured around those three pillars — positioning/messaging, launches, and sales enablement — with an emphasis on business outcomes (adoption, win rate, pipeline) rather than deliverables alone.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name and contact info, plus LinkedIn and a portfolio link if you have public messaging or launch assets to share.
2. **Summary** — 2-3 lines on product category, launch scope, and a headline GTM result.
3. **Core Skills** — GTM competencies and tools to establish strategic and tactical range.
4. **Professional Experience** — reverse-chronological, each role anchored in launch outcomes, enablement impact, and competitive positioning.
5. **Selected Launches** (optional) — 2-3 notable product launches with a one-line result each.
6. **Education** — degree and institution.

## Key Competency Areas

- Positioning and messaging development, validated through customer research or win/loss analysis
- Product launch planning and execution, tied to adoption or revenue metrics
- Sales enablement: battlecards, playbooks, and training that measurably improve win rate or deal velocity
- Competitive intelligence and market/category analysis
- Cross-functional GTM leadership across Product, Sales, Demand Gen, and Customer Success
- Buyer/persona research and voice-of-customer synthesis
- Pricing and packaging input based on market and competitive data
- Content creation for the mid-to-bottom funnel (case studies, demos, sales decks)
- Analyst and press relations, where PMM supports the external product narrative

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **Action + Scope + Measurable Impact**: name the GTM motion (launch, positioning refresh, enablement program), state the product or team scope, and close with an adoption, win-rate, or revenue metric. PMM bullets should demonstrate strategic thinking, not just asset production.

- Led the go-to-market launch for [product/feature], driving [X% adoption] within [Y days] of release
- Repositioned [product] against [competitor/category], contributing to a [X% increase] in competitive win rate
- Built a sales enablement program (battlecards, certifications, playbooks) that improved rep win rate from [X% to Y%]
- Reduced average sales cycle by [X days] by equipping reps with objection-handling and ROI messaging
- Conducted [X] win/loss interviews, surfacing insights that informed a messaging shift adopted across [Y] campaigns
- Partnered with Product to launch [X features/year], generating [$Y in pipeline] tied to launch campaigns

## Tips for Product Marketing Manager Resumes

- Show the outcome of your positioning and messaging work (win rate, adoption), not just that you "developed messaging."
- Quantify sales enablement impact wherever possible — it's often the most persuasive evidence of cross-functional influence.
- Name the launches you owned and their scale (feature vs. flagship product) so reviewers can gauge scope.
- Reference win/loss or customer research explicitly if you conducted it; it signals a research-driven approach to positioning.
- If you don't have hard revenue attribution, use adoption, engagement, or enablement metrics instead — just avoid vague claims.
- Keep tactical deliverables (decks, one-pagers, battlecards) in service of a business result rather than as standalone bullets.
