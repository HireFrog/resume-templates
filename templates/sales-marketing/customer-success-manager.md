# Customer Success Manager Resume Template

> **Format:** Markdown (`.md`) · **Designed for:** Humans + AI agents

*For post-sale professionals responsible for onboarding, adoption, retention, and expansion within an existing customer base.*

Customer Success Manager (CSM) resumes are evaluated on retention math: how much revenue you kept, how much you grew, and how efficiently you did it across a book of accounts. This template centers on renewal rate, net/gross revenue retention, and expansion revenue, while also making room for the relationship and product-adoption work that drives those numbers — since CS hiring managers want both the outcomes and the process behind them.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name and contact info, plus LinkedIn given the relationship-heavy nature of the role.
2. **Summary** — 2-3 lines on book-of-business size, retention/NRR performance, and account segment (SMB, mid-market, enterprise).
3. **Core Skills** — CS platforms, methodologies, and cross-functional skills for keyword matching.
4. **Professional Experience** — reverse-chronological, each role centered on retention, expansion, and adoption metrics.
5. **Certifications** (optional) — Gainsight, ChurnZero, or CS-specific credentials.
6. **Education** — degree and institution.

## Key Competency Areas

- Net revenue retention (NRR) and gross revenue retention (GRR) across an owned book of business
- Renewal rate and churn reduction, including proactive risk identification and save strategies
- Expansion revenue through upsell and cross-sell, tied to specific account growth
- Time-to-value and onboarding efficiency for new customers
- Customer health scoring and quarterly business review (QBR) cadence
- NPS/CSAT performance and relationship depth across multiple stakeholders per account
- Cross-functional escalation management with Support, Product, and Engineering
- Playbook and process design that scales across a growing account portfolio
- Renewal process ownership, including negotiation and multi-year contract renewals

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **Action + Scope + Measurable Impact**: name the CS motion (onboarding, QBR, save play, expansion), state the book size or account segment it applied to, and end with a retention, revenue, or satisfaction metric.

- Managed a book of [X accounts / $Y ARR], achieving [Z% net revenue retention] against a company target of [W%]
- Reduced churn from [X% to Y%] by implementing a proactive health-scoring and early-warning system
- Grew expansion revenue by [$X] ([Y%] of book) through upsell conversations tied to usage data and QBRs
- Cut time-to-first-value from [X to Y days] by redesigning the onboarding playbook for [segment]
- Maintained a [X NPS / Y% CSAT] score across [Z] accounts through structured quarterly business reviews
- Recovered [X% of at-risk accounts] flagged as churn risk through targeted save plays and executive escalation
- Renewed [X% of contracts] on time or early across a book of [$Y ARR], including [Z] multi-year renewals

## Tips for Customer Success Manager Resumes

- Lead with NRR/GRR and renewal rate — these are the metrics CS leaders scan for first.
- Separate retention (kept revenue) from expansion (grown revenue) in your bullets; conflating them weakens the signal.
- Quantify your book of business (account count and ARR) so reviewers can judge the scale you operated at.
- Include time-to-value or onboarding metrics if you own onboarding — it shows impact beyond just renewals.
- Name specific save plays or escalation processes you built, not just that you "reduced churn."
- If you support multiple segments (SMB vs. enterprise), clarify which one each metric applies to for an accurate read.
- Distinguish high-touch (fewer, larger accounts) from tech-touch/pooled (many smaller accounts) experience; the skill sets read differently.
