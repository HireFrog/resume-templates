# Growth Marketing Manager Resume Template

> **Format:** Markdown (`.md`) · **Designed for:** Humans + AI agents

*For data-driven marketers who own the acquisition funnel end-to-end, running experiments to improve conversion, CAC, and LTV across paid and organic channels.*

Growth Marketing resumes are evaluated on experimentation rigor and unit economics: can you run a structured test-and-learn process, and do your channels actually pay back. This template is built around funnel metrics, CAC/LTV, and A/B testing velocity, with an emphasis on showing the process (hypothesis, test, result) behind each win rather than just the final number — because growth hiring managers want to see how you think, not just what you achieved.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name and contact info, plus LinkedIn and a portfolio/dashboard link if you can share experiment results publicly.
2. **Summary** — 2-3 lines on channel/funnel ownership, CAC:LTV performance, and a headline growth metric.
3. **Core Skills** — experimentation, analytics, and paid/lifecycle channel tools for keyword matching.
4. **Professional Experience** — reverse-chronological, each role anchored in funnel conversion, CAC/LTV, and experiment velocity.
5. **Selected Experiments** (optional) — 2-3 standout tests with hypothesis and result, showing your testing process directly.
6. **Education** — degree and institution.

## Key Competency Areas

- Full-funnel ownership from acquisition through activation, retention, and referral (AARRR/pirate metrics)
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC) and lifetime value (LTV), including CAC:LTV ratio and payback period
- A/B and multivariate testing velocity, with statistically sound experiment design
- Conversion rate optimization (CRO) across landing pages, onboarding, and checkout flows
- Paid acquisition management across channels (search, social, programmatic) with ROAS accountability
- Retention and lifecycle marketing (email, push, in-app) to reduce churn and increase repeat usage
- Analytics and tooling fluency (SQL, GA4, Amplitude/Mixpanel, experimentation platforms)
- Cross-functional partnership with Product and Engineering to ship growth experiments

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **Action + Scope + Measurable Impact**: name the experiment or channel, state the funnel stage or audience it touched, and close with a conversion, CAC, LTV, or revenue metric. Where possible, include the hypothesis-to-result arc to show experimentation rigor.

- Reduced CAC from [$X to $Y] ([Z%] decrease) by reallocating paid spend toward higher-LTV channels
- Improved sign-up-to-activation conversion from [X% to Y%] through a [Z]-test onboarding experimentation program
- Ran [X A/B tests/quarter] on the acquisition funnel, with [Y%] statistically significant wins improving conversion by [Z%]
- Increased LTV:CAC ratio from [X:1 to Y:1] by launching a lifecycle email program that lifted 90-day retention by [Z%]
- Scaled paid acquisition spend from [$X to $Y/month] while maintaining ROAS above [Z:1]
- Cut checkout/signup drop-off by [X%] through iterative landing page and form-flow experiments

## Tips for Growth Marketing Manager Resumes

- Anchor your resume in CAC, LTV, and conversion rate — these are the metrics growth hiring managers scan for first.
- Show experiment velocity (tests run per quarter/month) alongside win rate; both speed and rigor matter.
- Use the hypothesis-to-result structure in at least a few bullets or a Selected Experiments section — it demonstrates your actual process, not just outcomes.
- Name specific tools (SQL, Amplitude, Mixpanel, Optimizely) since growth roles are often technically screened.
- Distinguish paid-channel wins (CAC, ROAS) from product/lifecycle wins (activation, retention) so scope is clear.
- Avoid vanity metrics like raw traffic or impressions unless tied to a downstream conversion or revenue outcome.
