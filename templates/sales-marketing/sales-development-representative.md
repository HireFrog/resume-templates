# Sales Development Representative (SDR) Resume Template

*For entry-to-mid-level sales professionals responsible for outbound prospecting, lead qualification, and building pipeline for Account Executives.*

SDR roles are activity- and conversion-driven: hiring managers want to see proof that you can consistently generate qualified meetings, not just that you made calls. Because many SDR candidates are early in their careers, this resume leans harder on measurable activity metrics and conversion rates than on tenure, and gives room to show tool fluency and coachability alongside the numbers.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name and contact info; include LinkedIn since SDR hiring often happens through social sourcing.
2. **Summary** — 2-3 lines on your outbound focus, tools used, and standout conversion metric.
3. **Core Skills** — prospecting tools, outreach channels, and CRM platforms to pass keyword screens.
4. **Professional Experience** — reverse-chronological, each role anchored in activity volume and qualified-meeting conversion.
5. **Certifications & Training** (optional) — sales bootcamps, Sandler/Winning by Design SDR training, HubSpot/Salesforce certifications.
6. **Education** — degree and institution; especially relevant for candidates transitioning from another field.

## Key Competency Areas

- Outbound prospecting volume across calls, emails, and social channels (e.g., LinkedIn)
- Meeting-to-opportunity and opportunity-to-close conversion rates for sourced pipeline
- Pipeline value generated and quota attainment against a booked-meetings or pipeline-dollar target
- Sequence/cadence design and multi-touch outreach strategy
- Discovery and qualification skills (BANT, MEDDIC, or similar frameworks)
- Tool fluency: CRM (Salesforce/HubSpot), sales engagement platforms (Outreach/Salesloft), and prospecting tools (ZoomInfo/Apollo/LinkedIn Sales Navigator)
- Collaboration with Account Executives on handoff quality and territory strategy
- Resilience and consistency — sustained activity levels over time, not just peak weeks
- Research and prioritization within a target account list or ideal customer profile

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **Action + Scope + Measurable Impact**: name the outbound motion, quantify the volume or segment, and end with a conversion rate, meeting count, or pipeline dollar figure. SDR resumes benefit from precise numbers since the role itself is metrics-driven.

- Booked [X qualified meetings/month] against a quota of [Y], sourcing [$Z in pipeline] for the AE team
- Achieved [X% meeting-to-opportunity conversion rate], outperforming team average of [Y%]
- Executed [X outbound touches/week] across cold calls, email, and LinkedIn using a [Z]-step cadence
- Ranked [top X] of [Y] SDRs for pipeline generated in [quarter/year]
- Partnered with [N] Account Executives to refine ideal customer profile, improving lead quality and lifting close rate by [X%]
- Onboarded and ramped to full quota in [X weeks], ahead of the team's average ramp time of [Y weeks]
- Maintained a [X%] response rate on outbound email by personalizing [N] sequences per target segment

## Tips for Sales Development Representative Resumes

- Lead with meetings booked and conversion rate — these matter more to SDR managers than years of experience.
- If you're early-career, translate any customer-facing, quota-carrying, or cold-outreach experience (retail, fundraising, campus roles) into the same metrics-first language.
- Name your tools explicitly (Outreach, Salesloft, ZoomInfo, Salesforce) — SDR job posts are often filtered by tool familiarity.
- Show ramp speed if you have it; hiring managers value SDRs who get productive fast.
- Mention specific outbound channels (cold call, email, LinkedIn, video prospecting) rather than a generic "outreach" — it shows range.
- Keep this to one page; SDR is an early-career role and recruiters expect concise resumes.
- If you're targeting a specific segment or vertical, mention it — SDR teams increasingly hire for account-based, segment-specific experience.
