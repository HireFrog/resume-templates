# Sales Manager Resume Template

*For sales leaders who build, coach, and manage a team of Account Executives or SDRs against a shared revenue target.*

Sales Manager resumes need to prove two things at once: that you can hit a number as a leader, and that you can develop the people who hit it with you. This template balances team-level revenue outcomes (quota attainment, forecast accuracy) with people-management evidence (hiring, ramp time, coaching, retention), since hiring committees weigh both heavily when evaluating first-line and second-line sales leaders.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name and contact info, plus LinkedIn given the relationship-driven nature of sales leadership hiring.
2. **Summary** — 2-3 lines on team size managed, quota owned, and overall attainment record.
3. **Leadership & Sales Skills** — management competencies and sales tools/methodology in one section, since both are screened for.
4. **Professional Experience** — reverse-chronological, blending team performance metrics with people-development outcomes.
5. **Individual Contributor Highlights** (optional) — prior AE/SDR track record if you were promoted from an IC role; establishes credibility.
6. **Education** — degree and institution.

## Key Competency Areas

- Team-level quota attainment and consistency across multiple quarters or years
- Hiring, onboarding, and ramp-time reduction for new reps
- Coaching methodology and its measurable effect on rep performance (e.g., win rate, average deal size)
- Forecasting accuracy and pipeline coverage management
- Territory design, quota-setting, and compensation-plan input
- Rep retention and internal promotion rates
- Cross-functional partnership with Marketing, RevOps, and Customer Success
- Use of sales tools and dashboards to drive team accountability (CRM, conversation intelligence, forecasting tools)
- Involvement in strategic or complex deal cycles as an escalation point for reps

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **Action + Scope + Measurable Impact**: describe the leadership action, name the team size or territory it covered, and close with a team performance or people-development metric. Favor team-wide numbers over individual deal stories to signal you operate at the manager altitude.

- Led a team of [X reps] to [Y% of quota] attainment across [Z consecutive quarters]
- Reduced new-hire ramp time from [X to Y months] by building a structured 30/60/90 onboarding program
- Improved team win rate from [X% to Y%] through weekly deal-review coaching and pipeline inspection
- Grew team-managed pipeline coverage from [Xx to Yx] of quota, improving forecast accuracy to within [Z%]
- Hired and developed [X reps], with [Y] promoted internally to senior or leadership roles
- Cut voluntary rep attrition from [X% to Y%] by implementing a coaching and career-pathing framework
- Redesigned territory and quota structure for a team of [X], improving pipeline coverage from [Yx to Zx]

## Tips for Sales Manager Resumes

- Report team-level numbers, not your own individual deals — reviewers want to see you operate a full team, not just close deals yourself.
- Quantify ramp time and hiring outcomes; these are often the most differentiating metrics between manager candidates.
- If you were promoted into management, say so explicitly and keep a short IC highlights section — it shows a track record that earned the promotion.
- Mention specific coaching cadences (1:1s, deal reviews, call shadowing) rather than saying "coached reps" — process detail signals real management experience.
- Include forecast accuracy if you track it; it's a strong signal of operational maturity for second-line leadership roles.
- Tailor team size and quota figures precisely — vague ranges read as less credible than specific numbers.
- Note span of control (team size, number of regions/segments) up top so scope is clear at a glance.
