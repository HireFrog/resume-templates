# Graphic Designer Resume Template

*For designers working across brand, marketing, print, and visual content.*

Graphic design resumes are judged on range, consistency, and the ability to serve a brief — whether that's a campaign, a brand system, or a one-off asset delivered under deadline. Because output volume and turnaround are real parts of the job, reviewers are also reading for reliability and scale, not just individual pieces of polished work. This template balances portfolio-first presentation with concrete evidence of impact and reliability.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name, contact, portfolio link.
2. **Summary** — your design focus (e.g. brand identity, marketing/campaign design, editorial/print).
3. **Skills** — tools and design disciplines.
4. **Experience** — reverse-chronological, framed around briefs delivered and their effect.
5. **Selected Work** — a short list of standout projects with links.
6. **Education** — degree and institution; brief, since the portfolio does most of the persuading here.
7. **Software/Certifications** *(optional)* — relevant tool certifications, if any.

## Key Competency Areas

- Visual craft: typography, layout, color, composition
- Working within and extending brand guidelines
- Delivering across formats (digital, print, social, presentation)
- Meeting deadlines and managing multiple concurrent briefs
- Collaborating with marketing, product, or creative directors
- Adapting style to audience and channel without losing brand consistency
- Producing at volume without sacrificing quality
- Taking feedback and iterating quickly through revision rounds
- Preparing production-ready files (print specs, exports, file handoff) without errors
- Maintaining a consistent visual voice across a high volume of varied requests

## Bullet Point Framework

Structure each bullet as **action + what you designed + outcome or scale of impact**. Since a single piece of work rarely comes with a hard performance metric, lean on scale (channels, audience size, asset volume, turnaround time) to make the impact concrete, and always tie the work back to the brief it was solving for.

- Designed [campaign/asset set] for [launch/event], used across [N] channels reaching [audience size]
- Refreshed [brand element] as part of a rebrand, applied consistently across [N] touchpoints
- Produced [N] assets per [month/quarter] for [marketing/product] team, consistently meeting tight turnaround deadlines
- Redesigned [collateral, e.g. sales deck/onboarding materials], improving [metric, e.g. engagement, clarity feedback]
- Partnered with [team] to establish visual guidelines for [channel], adopted as the team standard
- Delivered [N] concepts for [campaign/client] within [timeframe], with [X%] approved on first review
- Managed the visual production pipeline for [publication/channel], delivering [N] pieces per [period] with zero missed deadlines

## Tips for Graphic Designer Resumes

- Lead with your portfolio — for this role, it will be checked before or instead of reading every bullet.
- Show range if you have it (brand, digital, print), but lead with the type of work the job actually needs.
- Mention scale (channels, audience size, asset volume) to make impact concrete even without hard metrics.
- Reference turnaround and reliability under deadline — it's a real differentiator in this field.
- If you've worked with clients or stakeholders directly, mention it — brief intake and revision handling are real skills.
- Keep descriptions specific to the brief and outcome, not just the software used.
- If you work across brand, digital, and print, show at least one example of each — versatility is a real asset in this field.
