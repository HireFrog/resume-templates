# Senior Product Designer Resume Template

> **Format:** Markdown (`.md`) · **Designed for:** Humans + AI agents

*For designers who lead complex projects, mentor others, and shape design direction beyond their own screens.*

At the senior level, a resume needs to show scope beyond individual execution: ambiguous problems you framed for others, designers you mentored, and design decisions that shaped the broader product or system. Reviewers are reading for altitude — did you own a surface area, influence strategy, and raise the bar for people around you, not just ship well-crafted screens. This template is built around that shift.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name, contact, portfolio link.
2. **Summary** — 2-3 lines establishing scope and seniority (e.g. "leads design for X surface area," "mentors a team of N designers").
3. **Skills** — craft, strategic, and leadership skills.
4. **Experience** — reverse-chronological, weighted toward scope and influence.
5. **Selected Projects** — 1-2 flagship case studies with links, chosen for scope and influence, not just polish.
6. **Education** — degree and institution; brief, since this stage is judged on scope and outcomes, not credentials.
7. **Speaking/Writing** *(optional)* — talks, articles, or panels, if relevant to the seniority you're signaling.

## Key Competency Areas

- Framing ambiguous 0-to-1 problems and setting design direction for a team
- Leading design across a feature area, product line, or cross-functional initiative
- Mentoring or informally leading other designers and raising craft standards
- Influencing product strategy through design input, not just executing against it
- Driving design system or process improvements at a team or org level
- Balancing craft quality with delivery speed and business constraints
- Representing design in cross-functional and leadership conversations
- Making tradeoff calls with incomplete information and owning the outcome
- Setting design quality bars and processes that scale beyond your own work
- Partnering with design leadership on hiring, org structure, or team growth

## Bullet Point Framework

Structure each bullet as **action + scope + measurable or strategic outcome**, and make ownership explicit — name the team size, surface area, or number of stakeholders involved. At this level, vague scope ("worked on the redesign") reads as junior; specific scope ("led design for a team of 4 across the entire growth surface") reads as senior.

- Led design for [initiative], defining direction for a team of [N] designers/engineers, shipped in [timeframe]
- Owned end-to-end design strategy for [product area], driving [metric] improvement of [X%]
- Mentored [N] designers, raising the team's craft bar as reflected in [design review outcome]
- Partnered with product and eng leadership to reframe [problem], shifting roadmap priority toward [outcome]
- Drove adoption of [design system/process change] across [N] teams, reducing design debt by [X%]
- Established [a design practice, e.g. critique format, research cadence], adopted org-wide across [N] teams
- Represented design in [leadership forum, e.g. quarterly planning], shaping the decision to [strategic outcome]
- Scaled [process/system] from [team] to [N] teams, establishing it as company-wide practice

## Tips for Senior Product Designer Resumes

- Show influence beyond your own deliverables — what did you shape for the team or product that outlasted a single project?
- Name the scope explicitly (team size, surface area, number of stakeholders) so seniority reads clearly at a glance.
- Include at least one bullet about mentoring or leading other designers, even informally.
- Balance strategic bullets with at least one strong craft example — seniority doesn't mean the portfolio matters less.
- Reference a moment you influenced roadmap or strategy, not just execution, if you have one.
- Avoid title inflation; let scope and outcomes establish seniority rather than adjectives.
- If you've represented design in leadership or planning conversations, include it — it signals strategic trust, not just craft trust.
