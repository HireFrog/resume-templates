# UX Designer Resume Template

*For designers focused on interaction flows, usability, and the structure behind an experience.*

UX-focused roles are judged on how clearly you can reason about user needs and translate them into flows and interactions that actually work. Reviewers are looking for evidence of process — how you got from a problem to a structure, and how you knew the structure was right — not just finished screens. This template emphasizes that reasoning alongside execution.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name, contact, portfolio link.
2. **Summary** — your UX focus (e.g. complex workflows, B2B software, mobile).
3. **Skills** — research methods, tools, interaction/IA skills.
4. **Experience** — outcomes-first, reverse-chronological.
5. **Selected Projects** — case studies with links, if experience is limited.
6. **Education** — degree and institution; brief, since your case studies carry the structural reasoning.
7. **Certifications** *(optional)* — include only if directly relevant to UX practice, e.g. accessibility or research credentials.

## Key Competency Areas

- User research and synthesis (interviews, usability testing, surveys)
- Information architecture and flow design
- Wireframing and interaction design
- Usability testing and iteration based on findings
- Simplifying complex, multi-step processes into clear paths
- Working within technical or business constraints without losing the user's needs
- Communicating UX rationale to PMs, engineers, and stakeholders
- Collaborating with visual/UI designers to hand off a sound structural foundation
- Recognizing when a flow needs to be rethought structurally versus polished visually
- Validating assumptions with real users before committing to a direction

## Bullet Point Framework

Structure each bullet as **action + what you designed or investigated + measurable outcome**. Lead with the user problem or the decision the work informed, since that's what distinguishes UX reasoning from pure production — the outcome should tie back to usability, completion, or clarity, not just "shipped."

- Redesigned the [flow] information architecture, reducing task completion time by [X%] in usability testing
- Conducted [N] user interviews to identify pain points in [process], informing a redesign that reduced [metric]
- Simplified [multi-step flow] from [N] steps to [N], increasing completion rate by [X%]
- Ran usability tests on [feature] with [N] participants, surfacing [finding] that shaped the final design
- Partnered with engineering to scope [feature], balancing user needs against [technical constraint]
- Mapped the end-to-end journey for [process], identifying [N] friction points that became the next quarter's roadmap
- Consolidated [N] overlapping flows into a single unified pattern, reducing engineering build time by [X%]
- Restructured [navigation/IA], reducing reported user confusion around [area] by [X%]

## Tips for UX Designer Resumes

- Show your reasoning, not just your output — reviewers want to see how you got from problem to solution.
- Include at least one bullet that references user research directly informing a decision.
- If you don't have quantified results, use directional or qualitative outcomes ("reduced reported confusion around X").
- Keep visual polish out of the resume text — that's what the portfolio link is for.
- Tailor which projects you feature to match the complexity level of the role you're applying for.
- Name the flow or process explicitly (e.g. "checkout," "onboarding") rather than describing work in the abstract.
- Distinguish clearly between structural decisions (flows, IA) you owned and visual execution someone else handled.
