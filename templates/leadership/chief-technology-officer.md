# Chief Technology Officer (CTO) Resume Template

> **Format:** Markdown (`.md`) · **Designed for:** Humans + AI agents

*For technology executives who own company-wide technical vision and sit on or report to the leadership team and board.*

A CTO resume is closer to an executive bio than a traditional resume. It needs to establish that you can set technology as a competitive strategy, communicate that strategy to a board and investors, and be accountable for the technical foundation of the entire company — not just lead engineering delivery. Every section should reinforce that you think about technology at the level of company risk, investment, and long-term positioning, not just at the level of shipping features.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name, location, email, phone, LinkedIn.
2. **Executive Summary** — 3-5 lines: company stages you've operated at, the technical strategy you're known for, and a headline outcome tied to company success.
3. **Core Competencies** — technology strategy, board/investor communication, org building, technical risk management.
4. **Experience** — reverse-chronological, framed around company outcomes technology made possible.
5. **Board & Advisory** (optional) — board seats, advisory roles, speaking, patents if relevant.
6. **Technical Due Diligence & Exits** (optional) — a dedicated line if you've led diligence for a raise, acquisition, or exit.
7. **Education**.

## Key Competency Areas

- Setting technology as a competitive advantage, not just a delivery function
- Company-wide technical vision spanning product, platform, data, and security
- Board and investor communication on technical risk, investment, and roadmap
- Building and scaling the full technology organization (VPs, directors, and below)
- Vendor, build-vs-buy, and major architecture decisions with company-level consequences
- Technical due diligence for fundraising, M&A, or exit readiness
- Representing the company externally on technology (press, conferences, partnerships)
- Owning company-wide technical risk: security posture, compliance, and platform resilience
- Deciding when to build proprietary technology versus buy or partner, and defending that call to the board
- Setting the technical narrative that supports fundraising, enterprise sales, and analyst or press conversations

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **Action + Company-Wide Scope + Strategic Outcome**: what you set in motion, how much of the company's technical foundation it touched, and the business or strategic result. Every bullet should be legible to a board member or investor who doesn't know your stack — lead with the company-level consequence (revenue enabled, risk removed, capital preserved, time-to-market changed) and let the technical detail support it, not the reverse.

- Set technology strategy for a company scaling from [$X to $Y ARR / N to N users], building an organization of [N] across engineering, data, and security
- Led technical due diligence for a [$X] funding round / acquisition, resulting in [outcome, e.g. successful close with no material findings]
- Made the build-vs-buy call on [major system], saving [$X] and cutting time-to-market by [X%]
- Reported directly to the CEO and board, presenting technology strategy and risk on a [quarterly] cadence
- Rebuilt the technology organization following [event, e.g. rapid scale, acquisition], reducing critical incidents by [X%] within [timeframe]
- Established [security/compliance program], achieving [certification, e.g. SOC 2] ahead of [milestone, e.g. enterprise sales requirement]

## Tips for CTO Resumes

- Frame the whole resume around company outcomes technology enabled — revenue, valuation, fundraising, exit — since that's what boards and CEOs are hiring for.
- Name the company stages you've operated at explicitly (pre-seed through scale, or scale through exit); CTO experience is highly stage-dependent and mismatched stages are an easy pass.
- Include board and investor communication as a named skill, not an implied one — it's one of the most-screened-for and least-obvious CTO capabilities.
- If you've led technical due diligence for a raise or acquisition, state it directly; it signals you can withstand outside scrutiny of your technical decisions.
- Resist the urge to list technologies and languages at length; at this level, credibility comes from strategic judgment and outcomes, not a skills inventory.
- Be explicit about whether you built a technology org from scratch or scaled an existing one — both are valuable, but they're different stories and readers calibrate differently for each.
- State your reporting relationship and board involvement clearly (e.g. "reported to the CEO, presented quarterly to the board") — CTO scope varies widely and this removes ambiguity fast.
- Where relevant, note major build-vs-buy or vendor decisions by name; these are some of the highest-leverage, most memorable judgment calls a CTO makes.
