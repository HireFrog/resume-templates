# Director of Engineering Resume Template

> **Format:** Markdown (`.md`) · **Designed for:** Humans + AI agents

*For engineering leaders who manage managers and own outcomes across multiple teams.*

A Director of Engineering resume needs to show you can run an organization, not just a team: setting strategy across multiple workstreams, developing the managers under you, and being accountable for delivery at the org level. Reviewers are looking for evidence of scale — how many teams, how many people, how much of the business depended on what you ran. The resume should read as a story of org design and manager development, not a bigger version of an individual-contributor resume, and it should make clear that your primary lever for impact is other leaders' effectiveness, not your own hands-on work.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name, location, email, phone, LinkedIn.
2. **Executive Summary** — 3-4 lines: org size, scope, and a headline business outcome.
3. **Core Competencies** — org strategy, manager development, cross-functional leadership, budget ownership.
4. **Experience** — reverse-chronological, with org-level scope and outcomes per role.
5. **Selected Achievements** (optional) — 2-3 standout initiatives if the experience section runs long.
6. **Org Design & Team Structure** (optional) — a brief note on how you structured teams, if reorgs were a meaningful part of your impact.
7. **Education**.

## Key Competency Areas

- Managing and developing engineering managers, not just individual engineers
- Setting multi-quarter technical and organizational strategy
- Owning delivery across multiple teams or product lines
- Budget and headcount planning
- Partnering with peer directors/VPs in product, design, and go-to-market
- Driving org-wide technical initiatives (platform investment, reliability, developer experience)
- Representing engineering in leadership and planning forums
- Designing org structure — team boundaries, reporting lines, and ownership models that scale
- Coaching first-time managers into confident, effective people leaders
- Managing dependencies and sequencing across teams that don't naturally coordinate on their own

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **Action + Org Scope + Measurable Impact**: what you did, how many teams/people/managers it touched, and the business or technical result. At the director level, scope should almost always be expressed at the org level — "led X teams" or "managed N managers" — rather than in terms of a single team's output, since that's the dimension hiring committees use to calibrate the size of role you're coming from.

- Led an organization of [N] engineers across [N] teams and [N] managers, delivering [initiative] that drove [$X / X% business outcome]
- Restructured the org from [N] to [N] teams, improving cross-team delivery predictability from [X%] to [Y%]
- Owned a [$X] engineering budget, reallocating [X%] toward [platform/reliability] and reducing incident volume by [Y%]
- Promoted [N] engineers into management and developed [N] into senior/staff roles over [timeframe]
- Partnered with [Product/Design] leadership to launch [initiative], contributing [$X / X%] to [business metric]
- Drove adoption of [technical initiative] across [N] teams, cutting [metric, e.g. deploy time] by [X%]

## Tips for Director of Engineering Resumes

- Report scope in org terms — number of teams and managers, not just headcount — since that's the axis reviewers use to size the role you're coming from.
- Show you can operate through managers: cite outcomes you drove by developing and directing other leaders, not just by being close to the work yourself.
- Include at least one cross-functional or business-facing win; at this level, technical depth is assumed and business impact is what differentiates you.
- If you inherited a struggling org, frame the turnaround explicitly — what was broken, what you changed, what improved — rather than just listing the end state.
- Keep the resume to roughly one page per ten years of experience; directors are often judged as much on clarity and prioritization as on content.
- Distinguish org design work from delivery work in your bullets — reshaping how teams are structured is a distinct, valuable skill from simply shipping through an existing structure.
- Call out how you developed managers specifically, not just engineers; growing a first-time manager into a strong one is a core director deliverable and an easy differentiator.
- When listing scope, be precise about direct versus indirect reports (managers you supervise versus engineers under those managers) so readers can size your role accurately.
