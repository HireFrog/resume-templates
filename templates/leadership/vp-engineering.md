# VP Engineering Resume Template

> **Format:** Markdown (`.md`) · **Designed for:** Humans + AI agents

*For engineering executives who set org-wide strategy and operate as a peer to the rest of the leadership team.*

A VP Engineering resume needs to read like an executive document: strategy, scale, and business outcomes first, technical detail second. Boards and CEOs hiring for this role want to see that you've operated at the intersection of engineering and the business — budget, hiring plans, exec communication — not just that you can run teams well. The resume should demonstrate that you can own the entire engineering function as a business unit, with directors reporting to you and results measured in company-level terms.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name, location, email, phone, LinkedIn.
2. **Executive Summary** — 3-4 lines: company stage/scale, org size, and the headline business impact of your leadership.
3. **Core Competencies** — org strategy, executive communication, P&L or budget ownership, hiring leadership.
4. **Experience** — reverse-chronological, emphasizing company-level outcomes tied to engineering strategy.
5. **Board / Executive Exposure** (optional) — a short line if you regularly present to boards or investors.
6. **Selected Wins** (optional) — 2-3 company-defining initiatives you led, if the experience section runs long.
7. **Education**.

## Key Competency Areas

- Setting multi-year engineering strategy aligned to company strategy
- Owning engineering budget and headcount planning at the org level
- Hiring and developing directors and senior managers
- Partnering with the CEO, CPO, and other execs as a peer
- Communicating technical risk and investment to the board and investors
- Driving major company inflection points (scaling, platform rebuilds, M&A integration)
- Building and evolving engineering culture across a growing, multi-director org
- Owning engineering's contribution to company-wide OKRs and revenue targets
- Negotiating trade-offs between speed, cost, and reliability at the level the whole company feels
- Representing engineering in company-wide planning cycles alongside sales, marketing, and finance leadership

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **Action + Company-Level Scope + Business Impact**: what you did, how much of the org/company it touched, and the outcome in business terms — revenue, cost, risk, or valuation, not just technical metrics. At this level, scope should be described in terms of the whole engineering function (directors managed, total org size, percentage of company headcount) and impact should be traceable to something the CEO or board would care about.

- Built and led engineering (org of [N], [N] direct reports) through [stage, e.g. Series B to Series D], scaling the team [Nx] while maintaining [reliability/velocity metric]
- Owned a [$X] annual engineering budget, aligning spend to [strategic priority] and improving [efficiency metric] by [X%]
- Presented technical strategy and risk to the board [N] times per year, securing [$X] in additional platform investment
- Hired [N] directors/senior managers, building a leadership bench that reduced founder-dependency on technical decisions
- Led engineering through [company event, e.g. acquisition, IPO readiness, major outage], delivering [outcome]
- Partnered with the CEO and CPO to reset company strategy, resulting in [$X revenue / X% growth outcome]

## Tips for VP Engineering Resumes

- Open with company stage and scale (headcount, growth rate, funding stage) — it's the fastest way for a reader to calibrate whether your experience matches their context.
- Translate engineering work into business language: revenue enabled, cost avoided, risk reduced — boards and CEOs read resumes through that lens, not a technical one.
- Show a track record of building leadership benches, not just individual teams; VPs are hired partly to reduce key-person risk across the whole engineering org.
- If you've operated through a major company transition (funding round, acquisition, turnaround), name it explicitly — it signals you can operate under real pressure at the executive level.
- Keep technical detail sparse and purposeful; one or two lines establishing credibility is enough, the rest should be strategy, budget, and outcomes.
- Make your reporting line and peer group explicit (e.g. "reported to the CEO, peer to the CPO and VP Sales") — it helps readers place you correctly in an org chart.
- Quantify the size of the bet you owned — budget, headcount percentage of the company, or revenue dependent on your org — so readers can gauge the real weight of the role.
- If you've hired or replaced a leadership layer (directors, senior managers), state it plainly; building durable leadership structure is one of the clearest markers of VP-level impact.
