# Staff Software Engineer Resume Template

*For engineers whose scope spans multiple teams — technical strategy, architecture, and force-multiplying impact.*

Staff-level resumes are evaluated less on individual code output and more on organizational technical impact: what changed across teams because of a decision you drove. This template is built to surface that scope clearly, so a reviewer can immediately see the difference between "contributed to architecture" and "defined the architecture eight teams now build on." Keep every bullet anchored to a measurable org-level outcome.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name, location, email, GitHub/portfolio, LinkedIn.
2. **Summary** — 2-3 lines on your technical domain, organizational scope, and strategic focus.
3. **Skills** — architecture domains, systems, and technical leadership areas, not just languages.
4. **Experience** — reverse-chronological, with org-scale impact bullets rather than team-level task lists.
5. **Technical Leadership** (dedicated section) — initiatives, RFCs, and standards you drove, named explicitly as artifacts.
6. **Selected Talks/Writing** (optional) — internal or external talks, blog posts, or papers that demonstrate technical influence.
7. **Education** — degree, institution, year; brief, since scope of impact carries this resume.

## Key Competency Areas

- Cross-team technical strategy and architecture spanning multiple systems or orgs
- Driving org-wide technical standards, platforms, or shared infrastructure
- Identifying and resolving systemic technical risk before it becomes an incident
- Influencing technical direction without direct authority over the teams involved
- Mentoring senior engineers and raising the technical bar org-wide
- Balancing long-term architecture investment against short-term delivery pressure
- Writing technical strategy documents (RFCs, design docs) that other teams actually adopt
- Representing engineering technical direction to leadership and cross-functional partners
- Recognizing which technical bets are worth the organizational cost of pursuing them

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **action + org-wide initiative + measurable outcome**. Describe what you drove across teams — not just what you personally wrote — and quantify the scale of the change: number of teams, services, engineers, or dollars affected.

- Defined and drove adoption of [a service mesh architecture] across [8 teams], reducing cross-service latency by [30%]
- Authored [an RFC] establishing [a company-wide API standard] adopted by [12] services
- Led the technical strategy for [a platform migration] affecting [50+] engineers over [9 months]
- Identified [a systemic reliability risk] in [shared infrastructure], driving a fix that prevented [an estimated $X in incident cost]
- Mentored [6] senior engineers across [3 teams], several of whom advanced to staff-track roles
- Built consensus across [3 orgs] on [a technical direction], unblocking [a company priority initiative]

## Tips for Staff Software Engineer Resumes

- Scope every bullet at the org level — number of teams, services, or engineers affected — not just the team you sit on.
- Show influence without authority: staff engineers are judged on how well they align people who don't report to them.
- Include artifacts of technical leadership (RFCs, design docs, standards) as named accomplishments, not just "contributed to architecture."
- Balance strategic bullets with at least one or two concrete technical wins — staff engineers are still expected to go deep when needed.
- Keep the resume tight (1-2 pages); scope, not length, is what signals seniority here.
- If you've shaped hiring bars, leveling, or org-wide engineering practices, mention it — it's part of the staff-level signal.
- Avoid vague strategy language ("drove alignment," "influenced direction") without naming the concrete artifact or decision behind it.
