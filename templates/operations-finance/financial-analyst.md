# Financial Analyst Resume Template

> **Format:** Markdown (`.md`) · **Designed for:** Humans + AI agents

*For candidates who build models, analyze performance, and support financial decision-making.*

Financial Analyst resumes are judged heavily on precision — both in the work described and in the resume itself. Hiring managers look for evidence of technical modeling skill, business judgment, and the ability to communicate numbers to non-finance stakeholders. This template is built around demonstrating all three, with attention to the scale of the numbers you've worked with.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name, location, phone, email, LinkedIn.
2. **Summary** — 2-3 lines on your analytical focus (FP&A, corporate finance, investment analysis) and scope.
3. **Core Skills** — technical tools and financial competencies.
4. **Professional Experience** — reverse-chronological, with quantified bullets.
5. **Education** — degree, institution, year.
6. **Certifications** — CFA, CPA (in progress or complete), FMVA.
7. **Additional Sections** (optional) — notable models built, deals supported, or technical proficiency (Excel/SQL depth, financial systems).

## Key Competency Areas

- Financial modeling and forecasting
- Budgeting and variance analysis
- Data analysis (Excel, SQL, financial systems)
- Business partnering and executive reporting
- Month-end/quarter-end close support
- KPI tracking and dashboard development
- Ad hoc analysis for strategic decisions
- Scenario planning and sensitivity analysis

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **Action + Model/Analysis + Business Impact**: describe what you built or analyzed, and tie it to a decision, savings, or accuracy improvement. Precision matters here — round numbers consistently and make sure every figure you cite could hold up to a follow-up question in an interview.

- Built a [3-statement financial model] used to support a [$5M budget approval]
- Automated [monthly variance reporting], cutting close-related reporting time by [40%]
- Partnered with [department heads] on [annual budgeting], improving forecast accuracy from [±15%] to [±5%]
- Analyzed [pricing data across 20 SKUs], identifying a pricing change that increased margin by [3 points]
- Built a [dashboard in Power BI/Tableau] tracking [revenue/cost KPIs] for executive leadership
- Supported [due diligence] on a [$10M acquisition], modeling synergies and integration costs

## Tips for Financial Analyst Resumes

- Be specific about the size of the numbers you worked with — budget size, deal size, portfolio value. Scale signals seniority in finance more than title does.
- Name your tools precisely: "Advanced Excel" is vague, but "built pivot-table-driven variance models with 15+ tabs" is concrete and credible.
- Distinguish forecasting/FP&A work from accounting/close work if you've done both — they read as different skill sets to hiring managers.
- If you've presented findings to leadership, say so explicitly — communication skill is a real differentiator at the analyst level.
- Keep numbers precise and consistent — a resume with sloppy math or inconsistent formatting undermines credibility for a finance role specifically.
- If pursuing the CFA or CPA, list the level or exam status; partial progress still signals commitment to the field.
