# Business Analyst Resume Template

> **Format:** Markdown (`.md`) · **Designed for:** Humans + AI agents

*For candidates who translate business problems into requirements, analysis, and recommendations that others act on.*

Business Analyst is one of the broadest titles in the industry — it can mean process analysis, systems requirements, financial modeling, or a blend of all three. This template focuses on the throughline hiring managers actually screen for: the ability to turn ambiguous business questions into clear, actionable recommendations backed by data. Naming your domain and the decisions your work informed matters more than listing every methodology you know.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name, location, phone, email, LinkedIn.
2. **Summary** — 2-3 lines on your analytical focus area and the kind of decisions you've informed.
3. **Core Skills** — tools, methodologies, and domain areas.
4. **Professional Experience** — reverse-chronological, with outcome-driven bullets.
5. **Education** — degree, institution, year.
6. **Certifications** (optional) — CBAP, Six Sigma, or similar.
7. **Additional Sections** (optional) — notable projects, tools you've implemented, or a portfolio link if your work includes visible artifacts like dashboards or process maps.

## Key Competency Areas

- Requirements gathering and stakeholder interviews
- Process mapping and gap analysis
- Data analysis and reporting (SQL, Excel, BI tools)
- Business case development and ROI modeling
- Cross-functional communication (translating technical to business and back)
- Project and change management support
- Documentation (BRDs, user stories, process flows)
- Facilitation of workshops and requirements sessions

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **Action + Business Problem + Measurable Outcome**: name what you analyzed or delivered, and tie it to a business result — cost saved, revenue enabled, time reduced, or a decision made. If the outcome was a decision rather than a hard number, say what was decided and why it mattered.

- Led requirements gathering for [a claims processing system redesign], reducing manual handling time by [25%]
- Built a financial model evaluating [3 vendor options], informing a decision that saved [$400K] over two years
- Mapped and streamlined [an onboarding process] across [4 departments], cutting cycle time from [10 days] to [4 days]
- Analyzed [customer churn data] and identified [2] root causes, informing a retention initiative that improved renewal rate by [6%]
- Authored [15+] business requirement documents that became the basis for [engineering/product] delivery
- Partnered with [finance, ops, and IT] stakeholders to align on a single source of truth for [a reporting metric]

## Tips for Business Analyst Resumes

- Name your domain explicitly — finance, healthcare, retail, SaaS. "Business Analyst" alone tells a recruiter almost nothing about fit.
- Show the decision your analysis enabled, not just the analysis itself. "Built a model" is weaker than "built a model that led to a $400K decision."
- List the tools you actually use daily (SQL, Excel functions, specific BI platforms) — many BA postings are tool-specific.
- If you've worked on both business and technical sides (e.g. writing requirements for engineering teams), call that out — it's a differentiator.
- Keep documentation-heavy work (BRDs, user stories) brief on the resume; save examples for the interview or portfolio.
- Distinguish process/operations analysis from systems/data analysis if you've done both — they signal different strengths to different hiring managers.
