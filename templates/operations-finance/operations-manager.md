# Operations Manager Resume Template

*For candidates who run day-to-day business operations — process, people, vendors, and systems that keep a company functioning.*

Operations Manager roles vary widely by industry, but hiring managers are almost always looking for the same underlying signal: can this person take a messy, manual, or inefficient process and make it reliable, measurable, and scalable. This template is built around that signal, with an emphasis on scope (team, budget, functions owned) and quantified efficiency gains. Because the title covers everything from warehouse operations to office administration, the resume also needs to state your specific domain up front so a reader isn't left guessing.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name, location, phone, email, LinkedIn.
2. **Summary** — 2-3 lines on your operational scope (team size, budget, function) and the kind of results you drive.
3. **Core Skills** — a scannable list of systems, methodologies, and functional areas.
4. **Professional Experience** — reverse-chronological, with metric-driven bullets.
5. **Education** — degree, institution, year (omit year if 10+ years into your career).
6. **Certifications** (optional) — Lean, Six Sigma, PMP, or industry-specific credentials.
7. **Additional Sections** (optional) — major process improvement projects, cross-functional initiatives, or tools implementations too significant to bury in a bullet.

## Key Competency Areas

- Process design and continuous improvement
- Cross-functional coordination (sales, finance, HR, supply chain)
- Vendor and contract management
- Budget ownership and cost control
- Team leadership and hiring
- Systems/tooling implementation (ERP, CRM, workflow tools)
- KPI definition and operational reporting
- Risk management and compliance

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **Action + Scope + Measurable Impact**: start with a strong verb, name the process or team you touched, and close with a number that shows the result. If you can name both a percentage and a dollar or time figure, use whichever is more concrete for the reader.

- Redesigned the order fulfillment process across [3] warehouses, cutting average turnaround time from [5 days] to [2 days]
- Managed a team of [12] operations staff and an annual budget of [$1.2M], coming in [8%] under budget
- Implemented [ERP system] company-wide, reducing manual data entry by [30%] and eliminating [2] full-time reconciliation roles
- Renegotiated contracts with [15] vendors, saving [$180K] annually without disrupting service levels
- Built a weekly KPI dashboard adopted by [3] departments, cutting reporting prep time by [6 hours/week]
- Led the operational integration of [an acquired business unit], onboarding [40] employees within [90 days]

## Tips for Operations Manager Resumes

- Lead with scope. Team size, budget, and number of locations/functions managed tell a hiring manager more in five seconds than a paragraph of description.
- Quantify efficiency gains wherever possible — time saved, cost reduced, error rate dropped. Operations roles are judged almost entirely on measurable improvement.
- Name the systems you've worked in (specific ERP, WMS, or CRM platforms). Many operations postings screen for tool familiarity directly.
- Show breadth across functions if you have it — operations managers who've touched finance, HR, and logistics are more competitive for senior roles.
- Avoid vague verbs like "helped" or "assisted." Use "owned," "built," "redesigned," "led."
- If you've managed through growth, a reorg, or a crisis, say so — operational resilience under change is a strong differentiator at the manager level.
