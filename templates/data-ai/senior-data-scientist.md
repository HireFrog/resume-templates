# Senior Data Scientist Resume Template

> **Format:** Markdown (`.md`) · **Designed for:** Humans + AI agents

*For experienced data scientists moving into roles with more scope, more ambiguity, and more responsibility for the "why," not just the "how."*

At the senior level, hiring managers assume you can build a model — what they're evaluating is whether you can set the research agenda, mentor others, and make the case for a data science investment to people who control budget. This template shifts the emphasis from individual analyses toward technical leadership, cross-functional influence, and the scale of decisions your work has shaped.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name, location, email, LinkedIn, GitHub/portfolio.
2. **Summary** — 2-3 lines establishing your domain expertise, scope of leadership, and the caliber of decisions you've influenced.
3. **Skills** — advanced modeling, tooling, and leadership/mentorship capabilities, grouped by category.
4. **Professional Experience** — reverse-chronological, emphasizing strategic impact and team multiplier effects, not just individual model output.
5. **Selected Projects / Talks / Publications** (optional) — internal or external work that signals thought leadership.
6. **Education** — degree, institution, year; advanced degrees are common but not always required at this level.

## Key Competency Areas

- Setting technical direction — choosing which problems are worth solving and which methods fit
- Advanced statistical and ML methods, applied with judgment about tradeoffs, not just capability
- Mentoring and leveling up junior data scientists and analysts
- Translating ambiguous executive-level questions into a research plan with a defined scope
- Influencing roadmap and resourcing decisions through data-backed argument, not just analysis
- Building or improving the team's experimentation infrastructure, tooling, or methodology standards
- Communicating tradeoffs and risk to executives, including when a model or experiment should NOT be trusted
- Cross-functional partnership with engineering, product, and leadership on high-stakes decisions

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **Action + Scope of Influence + Business-Level Impact**: emphasize the size of the decision, team, or organization your work shaped, not just the technical method. At this level, "I built X" is table stakes — the differentiator is "I changed how the org decides Y."

- Led the [pricing model redesign] initiative spanning [3 teams], resulting in [$X in annual revenue impact]
- Built and scaled the company's [experimentation platform/framework], increasing the org's test velocity from [X] to [Y] experiments/quarter
- Mentored [4] junior data scientists, [2] of whom were promoted within [18 months]
- Presented [causal analysis of a $XM initiative] to the executive team, directly informing the decision to [scale/sunset it]
- Established [a modeling standard/review process] adopted across [X] data science teams, reducing production model failures by [X%]
- Partnered with [engineering leadership] to define the technical strategy for [a new ML capability], securing [$X] in headcount/budget investment

## Tips for Senior Data Scientist Resumes

- Shift bullets from "I built" to "I led" or "I influenced" where honest — reviewers are screening for scope, not just skill.
- Quantify the size of what you influenced (team headcount, budget, revenue, org reach), not just the model's accuracy.
- Include at least one mentorship or team-building bullet with a concrete outcome — leadership is expected at this level even in IC roles.
- Show judgment, not just capability — a bullet about correctly killing a flawed initiative can be as strong as one about shipping a successful model.
- Name the executive or leadership audience you've presented to; it signals your comfort operating above the team level.
- Trim early-career bullets that no longer reflect your current scope — a senior resume should read senior throughout, not just in the most recent role.
