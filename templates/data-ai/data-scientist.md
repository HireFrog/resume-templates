# Data Scientist Resume Template

*For scientists who use statistics, modeling, and experimentation to answer business questions.*

Data scientist hiring managers want evidence of rigorous methodology and business impact together — not just modeling skill in isolation. This template balances technical depth with the outcomes your analysis or models drove.

## Resume Structure

1. **Header** — name, location, email, GitHub/portfolio.
2. **Summary** — 1-3 lines on your domain (e.g. growth, risk, personalization) and core methods.
3. **Skills** — languages, statistical/ML methods, and tools, grouped by category.
4. **Experience** — reverse-chronological, weighted toward business or product outcomes.
5. **Projects/Publications** (optional) — notable independent projects, Kaggle results, or publications.
6. **Education** — degree, school, field (advanced degrees are common and worth listing prominently in this field).

## Key Competency Areas

- Designing and analyzing experiments (A/B tests, causal inference)
- Building predictive or statistical models to answer specific business questions
- Translating ambiguous business problems into testable hypotheses
- Communicating uncertainty and results to non-technical stakeholders
- Working with large or messy real-world datasets
- Validating models against real outcomes, not just offline metrics
- Partnering with engineering to get analysis or models into production

## Bullet Point Framework

Use **Action + Method/model + Business or decision outcome**. The strongest data science bullets connect a technical method to a real decision or dollar figure.

- Built [model type] to predict [outcome], improving [business metric] by [X%]
- Designed and ran [A/B test] on [feature], leading to a [X%] lift in [metric] and a company-wide rollout
- Developed [statistical method/model] that identified [insight], changing [team]'s approach to [decision]
- Partnered with engineering to deploy [model] into production, driving [$X impact / X% improvement]
- Reduced [X%] false positive rate in [model], saving [team] [X hours/week] of manual review
- Analyzed [dataset] to quantify [effect], directly informing [strategic decision]

## Tips for Data Scientist Resumes

- Anchor every technical bullet to a business outcome — modeling technique alone doesn't tell a hiring manager why it mattered.
- Name your methods precisely (e.g. "difference-in-differences," "gradient-boosted trees") rather than vague terms like "advanced analytics."
- Show at least one experiment or analysis where the result changed a real decision, not just confirmed an assumption.
- Mention when your work made it into production, not just a notebook — it signals you can partner with engineering.
- If you have relevant publications or competition results (Kaggle, etc.), list them briefly rather than in exhaustive detail.
- One page is standard early-career; two pages are acceptable with a PhD or 8+ years of experience.
